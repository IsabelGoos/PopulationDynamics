# <span style="color: #17648a;">Population dynamics of stray dogs</span>

## <span style="color: #17648a;">Background and motivation</span>

I was pursuing a degree in <span style="color: #ba4a79;">Mathematics at the Universidad Nacional de Rosario (UNR)</span>, Argentina. I had already completed the first stage of the programme, equivalent to a Bachelor's degree (locally called Bachillerato), and had begun the Master's-level coursework.

The entire curriculum was strongly oriented towards <span style="color: #ba4a79;">pure mathematics</span>. This equipped me two important skills: a rigorous way of thinking and a solid foundation in mathematical methods. However, from my perspective, it also had a limitation: I was learning how to approach and solve mathematical problems, but had relatively little exposure to applying the associated methods to <span style="color: #ba4a79;">real-world challenges</span>.

## <span style="color: #17648a;">How the project started</span>

An interesting opportunity arose when Ada Seghesso, a CONICET researcher at UNR's <span style="color: #ba4a79;">Faculty of Veterinary Sciences</span>, gave a seminar at my faculty. 

Dogs are extremely popular pets in Argentina, but the country also faces a significant problem with stray dog populations. During her presentation, Ada discussed a study conducted in Coronel Bogado and Villa Amelia, where dog populations had been estimated twice: once before and once after a public castration campaign. The aim was to assess the campaign's impact on population dynamics.

Unfortunately, regularly conducting campaigns and carrying out the subsequent population surveys was financially difficult. This motivated a search for an alternative approach: developing a <span style="color: #ba4a79;">mathematical model</span> capable of describing <span style="color: #ba4a79;">stray dog population dynamics</span> and evaluating the potential <span style="color: #ba4a79;">impact of different castration strategies</span>.

## <span style="color: #17648a;">My contribution</span>

At the time, I was looking for a topic for my *tesina* (the equivalent of a Master's thesis), and this opportunity immediately caught my interest. For the first time, I had the chance to apply my mathematical background to a real-world problem with <span style="color: #ba4a79;">tangible value</span>.

This repository documents my journey through that <span style="color: #ba4a79;">modelling process</span>. It illustrates how a mathematical model can be built progressively, starting from the simplest possible representation of a system and gradually incorporating greater complexity to capture its essential dynamics. It also documents the challenges, decisions, and lessons I encountered along the way.

## <span style="color: #17648a;">From pure mathematics to mathematical modelling</span>

Moving from pure mathematics to applied modelling required a shift in mindset. I had to become more comfortable with approximation and <span style="color: #ba4a79;">creative problem-solving</span>, learning to translate mathematical methods into concrete representations of real-world phenomena, while maintaining the mathematical rigour that had shaped my previous education.

I hope this repository can be useful to students and professionals interested in mathematical modelling, particularly those making the transition from a predominantly theoretical mathematical background to applied problem-solving.
