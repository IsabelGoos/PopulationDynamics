# Population dynamics of stray dogs

## Background and motivation

I was pursuing a degree in Mathematics at the Universidad Nacional de Rosario (UNR), Argentina. I had already completed the first stage of the programme, equivalent to a Bachelor's degree (locally called Bachillerato), and had begun the Master's-level coursework.

The entire curriculum was strongly oriented towards pure mathematics. This equipped me two important skills: a rigorous way of thinking and a solid foundation in mathematical methods. However, from my perspective, it also had a limitation: I was learning how to approach and solve mathematical problems, but had relatively little exposure to applying the associated methods to real-world challenges.

## How the project started

An interesting opportunity arose when Ada Seghesso, a CONICET researcher at UNR's Faculty of Veterinary Sciences, gave a seminar at my faculty. 

Dogs are extremely popular pets in Argentina, but the country also faces a significant problem with stray dog populations. During her presentation, Ada discussed a study conducted in Coronel Bogado and Villa Amelia, where dog populations had been estimated twice: once before and once after a public castration campaign. The aim was to assess the campaign's impact on population dynamics.

Unfortunately, regularly conducting campaigns and carrying out the subsequent population surveys was financially difficult. This motivated a search for an alternative approach: developing a mathematical model capable of describing stray dog population dynamics and evaluating the potential impact of different castration strategies.

## My contribution

At the time, I was looking for a topic for my *tesina* (the equivalent of a Master's thesis), and this opportunity immediately caught my interest. For the first time, I had the chance to apply my mathematical background to a real-world problem with tangible social value.

This repository documents my journey through that modelling process. It illustrates how a mathematical model can be built progressively, starting from the simplest possible representation of a system and gradually incorporating greater complexity to capture its essential dynamics. It also documents the challenges, decisions, and lessons I encountered along the way.

## From pure mathematics to mathematical modelling

Moving from pure mathematics to applied modelling required a shift in mindset. I had to become more comfortable with approximation and creative problem-solving, learning to translate mathematical methods into concrete representations of real-world phenomena, while maintaining the mathematical rigour that had shaped my previous education.

I hope this repository can be useful to students and professionals interested in mathematical modelling, particularly those making the transition from a predominantly theoretical mathematical background to applied problem-solving.
